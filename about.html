Artist biography

About

John Akande (b. Lagos, Nigeria) is a contemporary Nigerian figurative artist whose practice explores human expression, cultural identity, music and the complexities of everyday life.

Born
Lagos, Nigeria
Education
Yaba College of Technology, Lagos
Medium
Acrylic on canvas, mixed media
Exhibited
Nigeria · Malta · Online

His artistic journey is rooted in his early exposure to the cultural richness of Lagos and a longstanding fascination with the ways people express, experience and connect with one another. He received his formal art education at Yaba College of Technology, Lagos, where he developed a foundation in visual art and figurative representation.

Working primarily with acrylic on canvas, alongside mixed-media approaches, John Akande combines elements of realism and expressionism to construct emotionally engaging visual narratives. His paintings are distinguished by expressive brushwork, layered textures, vibrant yet deliberate colour relationships and a strong sensitivity to composition. Through the human figure, he explores themes of memory, identity, relationships, belonging and the emotional dimensions of human existence.

Music holds a significant place in his practice, informed by his personal encounters with musical environments and his appreciation of its cultural and emotional significance. His paintings frequently examine musicians, musical instruments and shared moments of musical experience, using them as subjects through which to investigate human connection, heritage and the passage of personal and collective memories.

Drawing from Nigerian cultural experiences while engaging with universal human themes, Akande seeks to create works that resonate beyond geographical and cultural boundaries. His practice reflects an ongoing commitment to storytelling through painting, using the visual language of the human figure to communicate experiences, emotions and narratives that connect people across cultures.

His works have been exhibited in Nigeria and internationally, including Malta, and have featured in group exhibitions and online presentations.

In his own words

Artist Statement

“I see painting as a means of giving visual form to experiences that words cannot always fully express.”

My artistic practice explores the relationship between music, human expression, cultural identity and the experiences that shape everyday life. Working primarily through figurative painting, I am interested in the human figure as a vessel for stories, emotions and shared experiences. My paintings draw from observations of life, personal memories and cultural encounters, translating moments of human existence into visual narratives.

Music occupies a central place in my practice, not merely as a subject to be represented but as a language through which I explore human connection, memory, emotion and belonging. My early encounters with musical environments and the cultural significance of music in everyday life have shaped my interest in the relationship between people, instruments and the spaces they inhabit. I am particularly drawn to the gestures, expressions and quiet interactions that surround musical experiences, often exploring what exists beyond the performance itself.

Works such as Rhythm of Our Heritage (2026), Little Dreamer II (2025) and My Father’s Footsteps II examine different dimensions of this relationship. While some compositions capture the collective experience of music-making, others focus on individual moments of reflection, aspiration and rest. Through these narratives, I seek to reveal the emotional and human experiences that often remain beneath the surface of ordinary encounters.

Rooted in my Nigerian background and influenced by the richness of African cultural life, my work also reflects a broader interest in the human condition. I draw upon the visual language of my environment, combining elements of expressionism and realism through expressive brushwork, vibrant yet considered colour relationships, texture and carefully constructed compositions.

Ultimately, my practice is an ongoing exploration of how art can preserve memories, communicate emotions and create connections between people and cultures. I see painting as a means of giving visual form to experiences that words cannot always fully express, inviting viewers to recognise something of their own humanity within the stories I tell.

— John Akande
